# What Would You Build? Five Real-World Examples

*Written for business owners, not developers. Each example shows exactly what
you'd say, what the crew does, the few decisions you'd make, and what you end up
with. Every one of these follows the same rules: nothing goes live without
passing inspection, nothing irreversible happens without your yes, and you can
watch it all from your phone.*

---

## 1. The booking page that ends phone tag

**Who this is for:** any appointment business — salon, HVAC, dog groomer,
consultant, dentist.

**What you'd say:**
> "I want customers to book appointments on my website instead of calling. I
> take appointments Tuesday through Saturday, 9 to 5, and I need 30 minutes
> between jobs."

**What the crew does:** interviews you (services? deposit? cancellation
policy?), drafts a Blueprint you approve, then builds the booking page, the
confirmation and reminder texts, and a calendar view for you — testing every
flow by clicking through it on a phone-sized screen like a customer would,
checking it's accessible, and security-checking the whole thing before you see
it.

**Decisions you'd make:** approve the Blueprint; look at a private preview link
on your phone; say "yes, go live." Three moments, maybe ten minutes total.

**What you get:** bookings while you sleep, reminders that cut no-shows, and a
crew that keeps improving it — a monthly checkup might come back with "your
booking page takes 4 seconds to load on phones; we can make it under 2."

---

## 2. The instant-quote calculator that captures leads

**Who this is for:** contractors, landscapers, painters, movers, cerakoters —
anyone whose phone quotes take twenty minutes each.

**What you'd say:**
> "People always call asking 'what would it cost to…'. I want a page where they
> answer five questions and get a ballpark price, and I get their contact info."

**What the crew does:** turns your pricing logic into a step-by-step estimator,
builds the lead inbox that emails you each submission, and — because forms that
collect customer info are a security-sensitive surface — runs the deeper
inspection tier: spam protection, data handling checks, and a test that
strangers can't see other people's submissions.

**Decisions you'd make:** approve the Blueprint (including your pricing rules
in plain English), preview it, approve go-live. The crew will *ask before*
anything touches how prices display — money is always an owner decision.

**What you get:** a 24/7 salesperson. Every visitor either self-qualifies or
leaves you a warm lead with the job details already filled in.

## 3. One dashboard instead of five spreadsheets

**Who this is for:** any owner juggling orders, jobs, inventory, or crew
schedules across spreadsheets, texts, and sticky notes.

**What you'd say:**
> "Every morning I check three spreadsheets and my email to figure out what's
> happening today. I want one page that shows me open jobs, what's running
> late, and this week's numbers."

**What the crew does:** connects your existing sources, designs the morning
view around how *you* actually triage (that's the interview), and builds
alerts for the two or three things that should interrupt you. Because it's
internal, it goes behind a login only you and your staff have — with the
access rules tested automatically, not assumed.

**Decisions you'd make:** approve the Blueprint; decide who on your team sees
what; approve go-live.

**What you get:** a five-minute morning instead of a forty-minute one — and
because the harness keeps auditing, the dashboard gets faster and smarter over
time instead of rotting like the spreadsheets did.

---

## 4. The customer portal that stops "where's my order?" calls

**Who this is for:** shops with repeat customers — print shops, fabricators,
wholesalers, property managers, accountants.

**What you'd say:**
> "Half my calls are customers asking about order status or wanting a copy of
> an invoice. I want them to log in and see it themselves."

**What the crew does:** builds a simple portal — status, history, documents,
messages — and treats it as the highest-security tier automatically, because
customer data is behind it: login flows adversarially tested, a standing check
that customer A can never see customer B's orders, every change re-verified
before it ships.

**Decisions you'd make:** approve the Blueprint; approve anything touching
customer data handling (always brought to you); approve go-live per feature —
portals ship in small pieces, so you see progress weekly instead of waiting
months for a big reveal.

**What you get:** fewer interruptions, happier repeat customers, and an asset
competitors your size don't have.

## 5. The new revenue line: sell something online

**Who this is for:** any business with something sellable beyond walk-in work —
gift cards, classes, memberships, merchandise, digital downloads.

**What you'd say:**
> "I teach a workshop twice a month and it fills by word of mouth. I want to
> sell seats online — and gift cards too."

**What the crew does:** builds the storefront and checkout on top of a payment
provider (you approve which one), with the money tier's full treatment: every
payment path tested, refund flow tested, receipts verified, and **no code that
touches money ever merges without your explicit sign-off** — that rule is
hard-wired into the harness, not a courtesy.

**Decisions you'd make:** the most of any example, on purpose: approve the
Blueprint, the payment provider, the prices, the refund policy, and the final
go-live. Money decisions never batch, never auto-approve, never hide.

**What you get:** revenue that doesn't require answering the phone — plus the
monthly audit watching conversion, speed, and costs once it's live.

## The pattern behind all five

Same machinery every time, and it's the machinery — not the examples — that's
the point:

1. **You describe the outcome** in your own words. Ten minutes of questions.
2. **You approve a Blueprint** you can actually read.
3. **The crew builds in small pieces**, each tested, security-checked, and
   clicked through like a real user before you ever see it.
4. **You make only owner decisions** — money, going live, customer data. There
   are exactly sixteen reasons the crew is allowed to interrupt you, written
   down in [one page](docs/14-human-gates.md).
5. **It keeps getting better after launch** — monthly checkups, speed and
   security watching, and a crew that learns from every mistake permanently.
